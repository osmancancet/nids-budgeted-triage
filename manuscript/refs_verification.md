## Related-work gap summaries (entries 37-44)

Dimensions: **XD** = cross-dataset shift, **OS** = open-set / unseen attack families, **CAL** = probability calibration, **AB** = alert budget / SOC workload / fixed-FPR operation, **CP/SEL** = conformal or selective prediction.

**talpini2024enhancing (#37).** Talpini, Sartori and Savi argue that ML-IDSs must give accurate uncertainty estimates. They compare uncertainty-quantification methods and propose a Bayesian NN, testing on held-out "unknown" attack classes, i.e., OOD detection with open-set flavour. *Covers:* CAL/uncertainty and OS. *Does NOT:* evaluate cross-dataset transfer, use conformal guarantees, or relate uncertainty to an analyst alert budget.

**jin2026benchmark (#38).** Jin and Lee train and deploy across HIKARI-2021, CICIDS2017 and CICIoT2023 using harmonised payload-free features. They show that a threshold set for 5% FPR in-domain does not stay stable under strict transfer, and that good PR-AUC/ROC-AUC can hide threshold collapse or score-orientation inversion. *Covers:* XD, CAL (calibrated alerting) and AB (fixed-FPR operating point). *Does NOT:* handle open-set/unseen attack families (binary benign/attack only), or use conformal or selective prediction. Its orientation diagnostics are oracle-only.

**lew2026deployment (#39).** Lew, Sim and Cheong's mini review covers 29 empirical DL-NIDS studies from 2024-2026. It checks three deployment gaps (cross-dataset degradation, rejection of unseen attack families, and reliable confidence) and finds that none of the studies evaluates all three together. It proposes a unified evaluation protocol. *Covers:* XD, OS and CAL as review criteria. *Does NOT:* run experiments, or address alert-volume/analyst budgets, latency, or conformal/selective prediction.

**xin2025crossdataset (#40a).** Xin and Xu train a Transformer IDS with Platt-scaling calibration and AUC-oriented training on NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15 and CIC-IDS2017, within and across datasets. They report a 10-20% AUC gain over baselines in cross-dataset tests. *Covers:* XD and CAL (with ECE). *Does NOT:* address open-set/unseen families, alert budgets, or conformal/selective prediction.

**pal2025calibrated (#40b).** Pal, Islam and Liew propose a framework for confidence calibration and uncertainty estimation in deep IoT attack detectors, with a lightweight DL model, evaluated on IoT-23 for calibration, uncertainty and distribution shift. *Covers:* CAL, uncertainty, and OOD/shift within one dataset. *Does NOT:* test transfer across independent benchmark datasets, provide conformal coverage guarantees or alert-budget operation, or explicitly hold out attack families.

**li2026robust (#41).** Li proposes traffic-aware conformal prediction (ta-CP) for LLM-based (LLaMA3-8B, Qwen3-8B, Mistral-7B, plus XGBoost) intrusion detection. Calibrating on perturbed traffic restores the (1-α) coverage lost under adversarial covariate shift, and attack-orbit-invariant representations give pathwise coverage against adaptive attackers. It uses three IDS benchmarks, including RT-IoT2022. *Covers:* CP/SEL (empty sets mean abstain and escalate) and adversarial covariate shift. *Does NOT:* study natural cross-dataset shift, open-set/unseen attack families (it assumes known perturbable-feature masks), probability calibration such as ECE, or explicit SOC alert budgets.

**flood2024bad (#42).** Flood, Engelen, Aspinall and Desmet examine seven widely used NIDS benchmark datasets. They identify six "data design smells" with heuristics to measure them, and show how these introduce experimental bias through poor diversity, murky labelling and ill-defined generalisation criteria. *Covers:* dataset validity, and it motivates XD/generalisation concerns. *Does NOT:* propose or evaluate detectors, calibration, open-set rejection, conformal methods, or alert-budget operation.

**lanvin2023errors (#43).** Lanvin et al. document labelling and packet-capture errors in CICIDS2017, correct them, and show that detection performance changes significantly once they are fixed. *Covers:* data/label quality affecting evaluation. *Does NOT:* address cross-dataset shift, unseen attacks, calibration, conformal/selective prediction, or alert budgets.

**Additional 2023-2026 papers (#44):**
- **cantone2024cross (#44a, IEEE Access 2024).** Cantone, Marrocco and Bria find near-perfect in-dataset accuracy but near-random cross-dataset performance, driven by data heterogeneity. *Covers:* XD. *Does NOT:* cover calibration, open-set, conformal/selective prediction, or alert budgets.
- **farrukh2023detecting (#44b, MILCOM 2023).** An open-set IoT NIDS that uses image-like packet representations and clustering to flag unknown attacks, reporting about 88% detection of unknown attacks. *Covers:* OS. *Does NOT:* cover XD, calibration, conformal guarantees, or alert budgets.
- **yu2025industrial (#44c, IJIS 2025).** Open-set ICS intrusion detection using BiGRU with prototype learning, plus EVT/Weibull thresholds to reject unknown attacks. *Covers:* OS. *Does NOT:* cover XD, calibration metrics, conformal methods, or alert budgets.
- **apruzzese2024adversarial (#44d, AISec 2024).** Examines how blind problem-space adversarial perturbations interact with concept drift in ML-NIDS, using multi-year MCFP traffic. *Covers:* temporal drift. *Does NOT:* cover calibration, open-set rejection, conformal methods, or alert budgets.
- **yumlembam2024comprehensive (#44e, Information Fusion 2024).** Botnet NIDS with adversarial hardening plus a conformal-prediction layer that rejects uncertain predictions (ISCX, ISOT). *Covers:* CP/SEL (rejection). *Does NOT:* cover cross-dataset shift, unseen attack families, calibration metrics, or explicit SOC alert budgets.
